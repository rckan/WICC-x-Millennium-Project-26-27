# Research Questions

The main question breaks into three outcome areas (consumer behavior, business performance, employment) and one framing question (what counts as disruption).

## Main question
To what extent is the rise of agentic AI disrupting traditional service-based industries, and how is this transformation affecting consumer behavior, business performance, and employment?

## Sub-questions

### Defining the problem
- [ ] What makes an AI system "agentic" rather than just a recommender or chatbot?
- [ ] Which service businesses are *intermediaries* (aggregate, compare, broker), and why are they the most exposed?
- [ ] How will we measure "to what extent"? (Revenue shifts? Traffic? Adoption rates? Job postings?)

### Consumer behavior
- [ ] How many consumers currently use AI to search, compare, or transact? Is that number growing?
- [ ] Which tasks do consumers trust an agent with, and which do they not?
- [ ] What would make consumers switch from a platform such as Booking.com to an agent?

### Business performance
- [ ] Are intermediaries showing early signs of pressure (traffic, take rates, marketing spend, stock reaction)?
- [ ] How are incumbents responding: building their own agents, partnering, or blocking?
- [ ] Who captures the value when an agent completes the transaction?

### Employment
- [ ] Which service-sector roles are most exposed (customer service, agents/brokers, support)?
- [ ] What do forecasts (e.g. Goldman Sachs) project for displacement vs. job creation?
- [ ] Is there evidence of hiring changes already?

## Hypotheses (to test, not assume)
- H1: Pure-aggregator businesses face more disruption than businesses that own inventory or relationships.
- H2: Consumer adoption is limited more by trust than by capability.
- H3: Incumbents with proprietary data will integrate agents rather than be displaced.
